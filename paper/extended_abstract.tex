\section{Motivation}

Stanchions in a reception area make queuing the free-energy-minimizing policy. The same stanchions in a corridor have no such effect---the corridor context suppresses the queuing script entirely. The behavioral difference between environments is structural, not merely evaluative: some actions become categorically unavailable. We take this observation as a design principle. Can a material environment parameterize a robot's generative model such that context determines which scripts are assembled?

We formalize this within active inference \citep{parr2022active,friston2017active} and the variational approach to scripts \citep{albarracin2021variational}. The theoretical basis draws on Latour's inscription of behavioral programs in material artifacts \citep{latour1992missing} and the embedded normativity framework \citep{guenincarlut2023embedded}: norms are not internal rules but constraints distributed across agent and environment.

\section{Architecture}

The generative model is parameterized by four matrices. The $\mathbf{A}$-matrix (observation likelihood) drives perceptual inference over context from material cues---queue signs, social density, spatial layout. The $\mathbf{B}$-matrix (transition model) emerges from composing fragment-level primitives into a context-dependent topology. The $\mathbf{C}$-matrix encodes context-specific preferences over outcomes. The $\mathbf{D}$-matrix encodes prior beliefs over fragment--context associations.

Fragment activation is governed by a precision-gated sigmoid:
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{activate } f \mid c) = \sigma\bigl(\gamma_c \cdot (E[D(f,c)] - \tfrac{1}{K})\bigr)
\end{equation}
The precision $\gamma_c$ varies with context. Environments with clear affordances (reception: $\gamma = 6$) yield sharp, decisive gating. Environments with transient or ambiguous cues (corridor: $\gamma = 3$) yield flatter gating, allowing more fragments through. Under stochastic evaluation, each fragment is sampled from Bernoulli($P$)---a direct realization of action selection under uncertainty in the active inference formalism.

The $\mathbf{C}$-matrix encodes opposing goal structures. Reception assigns $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.99$ and $C_{\text{scene\_assessed}} = 0.20$. Corridor assigns $C_{\text{scene\_assessed}} = 0.99$ and $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.02$. Hospital assigns $C_{\text{ready\_for\_service}} = 0.99$ and $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.08$. The risk component of EFE, $-\ln C(o_\tau)$, therefore penalizes contextually inappropriate outcomes by up to 3.9 nats per primitive.

\subsection*{Learning}

Fragment affinities are learned via Dirichlet concentration accumulation rather than hand-specified. For each fragment in each context, the system measures the marginal EFE contribution by ablation (removing the fragment and measuring the difference). Per-fragment centering isolates the cross-context differential signal. The evidence scale $F_0$ is set by empirical Bayes (median cross-context SD). The $\mathbf{A}$-matrix recognizer is trained by variational free energy minimization over labeled percepts (30 epochs, sufficient statistics accumulation).

\section{Experiments}

We evaluate using 240 conditions $\times$ 30 trials each (7,200 total). Each trial applies context-appropriate sensory noise ($\sigma = 0.05$, $0.10$, $0.20$ for reception, hospital, corridor) and Bernoulli gating from the precision-weighted sigmoid. All variance is endogenous---no post-hoc jitter is added.

\paragraph{Environment shapes policy evaluation.} Context produces a large main effect on expected free energy: $F(2, 7197) = 279.2$, $p < 10^{-100}$, $\eta^2 = 0.072$ (Table~\ref{tab:anova}). When all six fragments are available, the reception--corridor separation reaches $d = 0.91$.

\paragraph{Effect size scales with model richness.} At $k = 5$--$6$ fragments (where gating has discriminative room), reception--corridor $d = 0.87$--$0.91$, corridor--hospital $d = 0.53$--$0.73$. At $k = 1$--$2$ (where gating has little to differentiate), $d \approx 0.1$. The scaling is predicted: a richer generative model offers more material for the $\mathbf{D}$-matrix to filter, amplifying context sensitivity.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 1: Material cue factorial (48 cond.).} $2^4$ cue combinations $\times$ 3 contexts. Context modulates which cues produce effects: stanchions activate queuing in reception and hospital, not in corridor. Spearman $\rho$(cue count, policy length) $= 0.82$ in corridor, $0.53$ in reception.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 2: Fragment scaling (189 cond.).} $\binom{6}{k}$ for $k=1\ldots6$, $\times$ 3 contexts. More fragments yield longer policies ($\rho > 0.86$) with monotonically increasing EFE. Slopes differ by context because gating reduces the effective fragment count.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 3: Context transfer (3 cond.).} Six fragments in each context, identical knowledge. Reception composes 9 primitives including staff engagement. Corridor: 8 primitives, no queuing, no engagement. Hospital: 9 primitives, patient queuing, no direct approach. Jaccard distance between reception and corridor primitive sets: 0.30. KL divergence of posterior weight profiles: 1.96 nats.

\section{Discussion}

Environment does not bias evaluation of a fixed behavioral repertoire. It determines which scripts enter composition in the first place. The effect is large ($d > 0.8$) when the agent has enough fragment material for context to differentiate, and vanishes at $k=1$ where gating is trivial. This operationalizes Akrich's inscription \citep{akrich1992description}---artifacts specify behavior---and gives Lefebvre's thesis a quantitative realization \citep{lefebvre1991production}: change the space, change the cognition.

Context-dependent precision produces a natural hierarchy of behavioral certainty. Reception (high $\gamma$) yields stereotyped, low-variance sequences. Corridor (low $\gamma$) yields variable, exploratory behavior. The variance is not noise; it reflects the agent's uncertainty about which scripts the environment licenses, expressed through the Bernoulli sampling mechanism.

The $\mathbf{A}$-matrix closes the perception--action loop. The robot infers context from quiet-zone signage, seated postures, and low arousal---not from a label. Recognition accuracy: 93.3\% across all conditions. Material cues shift the posterior, which reshapes the active fragment set, which changes the composed policy.

Context explains 7\% of total EFE variance ($\eta^2 = 0.072$). Condition---which fragments are available---dominates by design, since the experiment varies both. The between-context effect is reliable ($p < 10^{-100}$) and large where expected ($d = 0.91$ at $k=6$). Within-condition SD of 5.32 nats reflects real decision uncertainty at the gating boundary.

\paragraph{Limitations and next steps.} The simulated environments lack the perceptual richness of physical deployment. The $\mathbf{C}$-matrix is hand-specified (learning context preferences from interaction is an open problem). Hierarchical precision---tightening $\gamma_c$ as the agent accumulates experience in a context---would connect this work to meta-learning and epistemic foraging.
