\section{Motivation}

Stanchions in a reception area make queuing the free-energy-minimizing policy. The same stanchions in a corridor do nothing, because the corridor context suppresses the queuing script. The difference is not evaluative. It is structural: some actions become unavailable.

We ask whether a material environment can parameterize a robot's generative model so that context determines which scripts are assembled. The question is grounded in active inference \citep{parr2022active,friston2017active}, the variational script formalism \citep{albarracin2021variational}, Latour's analysis of behavioral programs inscribed in artifacts \citep{latour1992missing}, and the embedded normativity framework \citep{guenincarlut2023embedded}.

\section{Architecture}

The generative model has four matrices. $\mathbf{A}$ (observation likelihood) is a perceptual recognizer inferring context from queue signs, social density, and spatial layout. $\mathbf{B}$ (transition model) is assembled by composing fragment-level primitives into a topology. $\mathbf{C}$ encodes preferences over outcomes, specified per context. $\mathbf{D}$ encodes prior beliefs about which fragments belong to which contexts.

Fragment activation follows a precision-gated sigmoid:
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{activate } f \mid c) = \sigma\bigl(\gamma_c \cdot (E[D(f,c)] - \tfrac{1}{K})\bigr)
\end{equation}
The precision $\gamma_c$ varies across environments. A reception desk ($\gamma = 6$) has clear affordances and produces sharp gating. A corridor ($\gamma = 3$) has ambiguous cues and produces flatter gating, admitting more fragments. Under stochastic evaluation, fragments are sampled from Bernoulli($P$): action selection under uncertainty in the standard active inference sense.

The $\mathbf{C}$-matrix differs across contexts. Reception: $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.99$, $C_{\text{scene\_assessed}} = 0.20$. Corridor: $C_{\text{scene\_assessed}} = 0.99$, $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.02$. Hospital: $C_{\text{ready\_for\_service}} = 0.99$, $C_{\text{interaction}} = 0.08$. The risk term of EFE is $-\ln C(o_\tau)$, so contextually wrong outcomes cost up to 3.9 nats per primitive.

\subsection*{Learning}

Fragment affinities are learned from synthetic episodes by Dirichlet concentration accumulation. For each fragment in each context, ablation measures the marginal EFE contribution. Per-fragment centering isolates cross-context signal. The evidence scale $F_0$ is set by empirical Bayes to the median cross-context SD. The $\mathbf{A}$-matrix recognizer is trained by VFE minimization over labeled percepts (30 epochs).

\section{Experiments}

240 conditions $\times$ 30 trials $=$ 7,200 total. Each trial uses sensory noise matched to context ($\sigma = 0.05$, $0.10$, $0.20$ for reception, hospital, corridor) and Bernoulli gating from the sigmoid. No post-hoc jitter is added.

\paragraph{Main effect.} Context shapes policy evaluation: $F(2, 7197) = 279.2$, $p < 10^{-100}$, $\eta^2 = 0.072$ (Table~\ref{tab:anova}). With all six fragments available, reception vs.\ corridor gives $d = 0.91$.

\paragraph{Effect size scales with fragment count.} At $k = 5$--6, reception--corridor $d = 0.87$--$0.91$, corridor--hospital $d = 0.53$--$0.73$. At $k = 1$--2, $d \approx 0.1$. With few fragments, gating has little to differentiate. With many, the $\mathbf{D}$-matrix separates contexts.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 1: Cue factorial (48 cond.).} $2^4$ material cues $\times$ 3 contexts. Stanchions activate queuing in reception, not in corridor. Spearman $\rho$(cue count, policy length) $= 0.82$ in corridor, $0.53$ in reception.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 2: Scaling (189 cond.).} $\binom{6}{k}$ for $k=1\ldots6$, $\times$ 3 contexts. Policy length grows with $k$ ($\rho > 0.86$), as does EFE. Slopes differ across contexts.

\paragraph{Exp.\ 3: Transfer (3 cond.).} Six fragments, three contexts, identical knowledge. Reception: 9 primitives, staff engagement included. Corridor: 8 primitives, no queuing, no engagement. Hospital: 9 primitives, patient queuing, no direct approach. Jaccard distance of primitive sets: 0.30. KL divergence of weight profiles: 1.96 nats.

\section{Discussion}

Environment determines which scripts enter composition, not just how they are evaluated. The effect is large ($d > 0.8$) when the agent has enough fragments for context to differentiate. It vanishes at $k=1$, where gating is trivial. This is Akrich's inscription \citep{akrich1992description} made computational: artifacts specify behavior rather than suggest it. It is also Lefebvre \citep{lefebvre1991production} made quantitative: change the space, change the cognition.

Context-dependent precision produces a hierarchy of behavioral certainty. Reception ($\gamma = 6$) gives stereotyped sequences. Corridor ($\gamma = 3$) gives variable, exploratory behavior. The variance is decision uncertainty about which scripts the environment licenses, expressed through Bernoulli sampling at the gating boundary.

The $\mathbf{A}$-matrix closes the perception--action loop. The robot infers context from quiet-zone signage, seated postures, low arousal. Recognition accuracy: 93.3\%. Material cues shift the context posterior, which reshapes the active set, which changes the policy.

Context explains 7\% of total EFE variance. Condition (which fragments are available) dominates by design. The between-context effect is large where expected ($d = 0.91$ at $k=6$) and small where expected ($d \approx 0.1$ at $k=1$). Within-condition SD of 5.32 nats is decision uncertainty, not noise.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Simulated environments lack perceptual richness. The $\mathbf{C}$-matrix is hand-specified; learning preferences from interaction is open. Hierarchical precision (adapting $\gamma_c$ with experience) would connect to meta-learning.
